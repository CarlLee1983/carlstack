# Philosophical Engineering Writing

Use a philosophical concept to discover an engineering question and a practical way to examine it. The concept should change what the article asks readers to notice or decide.

## Workflow

1. Read `docs/content-guide.md`, the current content schema, and the closest published articles. Search `src/content/blog`, `docs/research`, and `docs/article-queue.md` by source URL, stable identifier, title terms, and concept terms before researching or creating a topic. Update an existing article when the source or core reader outcome is already covered; for a new article, state the distinct claim and reader payoff.
2. Select a philosophical concept because it helps expose a concrete engineering assumption, boundary, responsibility, relationship, or decision. When asked to brainstorm, compare a few concept-to-engineering mappings, then pursue the clearest novel one unless the user asks only for options.
3. Read the relevant primary text and a reliable scholarly interpretation. Record the work, section or page, translation, and any disagreement that affects the article. Explain the concept in its original context before applying it.
4. Make the bridge explicit: distinguish what the philosopher argues from the author's engineering inference. Show how the concept changes a system-analysis question, design decision, code boundary, test, or operational rule. Do not present the philosopher as having prescribed modern software practices.
5. Ground the argument in one concrete system scenario. Carry it from the initial problem through analysis and implementation to an observable acceptance condition. Add a check, decision rule, or next action readers can use.
6. State where the philosophical lens stops helping or leaves the technical choice undecided. Keep philosophical interpretation, sourced facts, and engineering recommendations distinguishable.
7. For CarlStack article structure, voice, frontmatter, diagrams, covers, formatting, and publication gates, apply the `carlstack-copywriting` skill and repository content guide. Do not restate their rules here.

## Completion

The article has a clear engineering claim, an accurate source-grounded account of its philosophical concept, an explicit and bounded application, a concrete reader action, and citations readers can follow.
